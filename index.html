Overview01

Artemis turns a technician's day into one guided flow — from the morning's work orders to the last status synced back to planning.

Maintenance technicians juggle radio calls, paper tickets, and after-hours data entry. Artemis puts the whole route in one place: what's next, clock in, log what happened, move on.

Every update — completed, on hold, resident away, parts needed — flows straight back to the planning team, so the day's schedule stays honest in real time.

Problem02

The day's work lives in too many places.

Friction adds up on every job.

Technicians lose time chasing the next address, re-explaining delays over the radio, and re-keying notes long after the last unit.

When a job stalls — no access, missing parts, resident away — planning finds out late, and the whole schedule quietly drifts.

Goals03
01

Give technicians one clear view of the day, in order.

02

Make clock in, clock out, and status a single tap in the field.

03

Capture the why behind every delay — not just the what.

04

Keep planning in sync without a single phone call.

Relayed the moment it happens.

Features05

Offline-first

Timers and logs work with no signal, then sync the moment it returns.

Guided route

Jobs ordered by time, priority, and location — no re-planning on the move.

One-tap clock

Start and stop time against the exact work order, never the wrong one.

Status with a reason

Structured holds planning can act on — away, parts, access.

Photo evidence

Before and after attached to the ticket in a couple of taps.

Auto-relay

Every change flows to the planning system in real time.

Reflection06

Design insight

Technicians don't want an app — they want fewer steps. Every screen had to earn its place or get cut.

Key learning

A status is only useful with a reason attached. "On hold" tells planning nothing without the why behind it.

Outcome

A single field flow that replaces radio calls, paper tickets, and after-hours data entry — and keeps planning in sync all day.
